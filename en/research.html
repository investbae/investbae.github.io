Research & Advisory

Research & Advisory

We study law, policy and institutions in venture capital and capital markets, working directly from statutes, case law, disclosures and official statistics.

01 — Services

What we research, how, and what we deliver

Scope, method and deliverables are agreed before each project starts. Fees and timelines are discussed separately according to the scope.

01Venture capital & policy finance

Subject
Funds of funds, policy funds and venture funds; venture investment institutions
Approach
Analysis of commitment and investment data and official statistics
Outputs
Policy research reports, empirical analyses

02Capital-market regulation & legislative reform

Subject
Capital Markets Act, Venture Investment Act, Commercial Act; fundraising and investor protection
Approach
Review of statutory text and effective dates, case law and disclosures
Outputs
Reform reports, reviews of draft amendments

03Digital finance & security tokens

Subject
Virtual-asset user protection, market surveillance, issuance and trading of security tokens
Approach
Comparison of domestic law with foreign legislation and international standards
Outputs
Comparative reports, policy recommendations

04Comparative & international financial law

Subject
US, EU, Japan and other jurisdictions; IOSCO and FSB standards
Approach
Research on official texts of each jurisdiction
Outputs
Comparative law reports

05Research-based policy advisory

Subject
Policy and institutional questions under review by an institution
Approach
Issue analysis based on prior research and primary sources
Outputs
Issue memos, advisory meeting materials

06Conference and seminar presentations

Subject
Academic and policy events
Approach
Presentation and discussion of research
Outputs
Presentation papers

Outside our scope

  • Legal representation, legal advice or legal opinions on individual matters
  • Investment advice or solicitation regarding any security or financial product, or structuring of investments
  • Representing the official position of regulators or commissioning bodies

Staffing and conflicts of interest

Research is led by Seung-Uk Bae. External researchers may participate in individual projects; they are not full-time employees of KVMI. Relevant outside roles of the lead researcher are disclosed to a prospective commissioning institution before a project is accepted, and a project is declined or its scope adjusted where such a role could impair the independence of the research.

02 — Process

From scoping to final report

  1. ScopingResearch questions, intended use, disclosure and confidentiality.
  2. Research plan and quotationScope, method, deliverables and schedule.
  3. Research and interim reportingReview of primary sources, with interim results shared.
  4. Final report and briefingDelivery of the report and, where requested, a briefing.

  • 2026
    Journal article

    Legislative Trends and Implications of Digital Asset Market Surveillance: A Comparative Analysis of the United States, the EU, Japan, and IOSCO–FSB Standards

    디지털자산 시장감시 체계의 해외 입법 동향과 시사점 — 미국·EU·일본 및 IOSCO·FSB 국제기준의 비교법적 분석을 중심으로

    Seung-Uk Bae — Sole author
    Commercial Cases Review, Vol. 39, No. 2, pp. 3–98 · The Korea Commercial Cases Association

    Starting from the finding that the market-surveillance provisions of Korea's Virtual Asset User Protection Act distribute surveillance among individual service providers, the article compares the US, EU and Japanese approaches and the IOSCO–FSB standards, and proposes a proportionate, phased transplant of integrated market surveillance into a proposed digital asset framework act.

    KCI record (Korean) (opens in a new tab) ↗DOI 10.36894/kcca.2026.39.2.003
  • 2026
    Journal article

    Revitalization of Securities-Based Crowdfunding Systems in Korea

    증권형 크라우드펀딩제도 활성화를 위한 제도 개선방안

    Seung-Uk Bae (first author), Soo Hyun Ahn (corresponding author)
    HUFS Law Review, Vol. 50, No. 2, pp. 255–302 · The HUFS Law Research Institute

    Diagnosing why Korea's securities-based crowdfunding, introduced by the 2015 amendment of the Capital Markets Act, has contracted since 2019, the article compares the US, UK, Japan and Singapore along seven regulatory axes and proposes seven reforms, from issuer eligibility and investment limits to a secondary market, alongside stronger investor protection.

    KCI record (Korean) (opens in a new tab) ↗DOI 10.17257/hufslr.2026.50.2.255
  • 2024
    Commissioned public policy research

    [Translated] A Study on the Appropriate Size of the Korea Fund of Funds: Focusing on Its Effects on Private Investment

    모태펀드의 적정 규모에 관한 연구 — 민간투자에 미치는 영향을 중심으로

    Seung-Uk Bae (principal investigator), 김규림 (co-researcher)
    Commissioned research report, National Assembly Budget Office · National Assembly Budget Office (NABO)

    A study commissioned by the National Assembly Budget Office. Using quarterly commitment data by investor type from 2005 Q1 to 2023 Q3, the researchers found no clear evidence that the Korea Fund of Funds crowded out private investment, but found that its crowding-in effect weakened after 2014. The views are those of the researchers, not NABO.

    US Virtual Currency Regulation and Implications

    미국의 가상통화 규제 및 시사점
